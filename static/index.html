🔥 Buoyancy vs. Diffusion PHYSICS

On Earth, hot gases rise due to density differences, creating vigorous natural convection. In microgravity, this upward draft vanishes. Oxygen transport shifts entirely to slow molecular diffusion or forced spacecraft ventilation.

🌐 Spherical Reaction Shells OBSERVATION

Without buoyant elongation, stationary flames in microgravity form calm, symmetrical spherical envelopes. Chemical reaction rates decrease, soot aggregates in outer boundaries, and cool flames can emerge.

🛡️ Spacecraft Mission Safety SAFETY

Smoke does not rise to ceiling detectors in space. Fires can quietly smolder undetected in hidden equipment racks until forced ventilation currents suddenly deliver fresh oxygen.

Scientific Principles of Microgravity Combustion

1. What is Microgravity Combustion?

Combustion is a coupled thermo-chemical process governed by fluid motion, molecular diffusion, and chemical kinetics. On Earth, gravity produces buoyancy because hot combustion gases are significantly less dense than cold surrounding air. In microgravity, this buoyancy-driven convective draft vanishes, allowing fundamental molecular diffusion and chemistry to be observed without gravity-induced distortions.

The Scientific Chain: Gravity → Density-difference Buoyancy → Natural Convection → Altered Oxygen/Heat Transport → Flame Geometry & Extinction.

2. Why Gravity Doesn't "Pull Oxygen"

Gravity does not directly attract oxygen molecules to a flame. Instead, gravity exerts a downward body force on all gas molecules proportional to density ($\rho g$). Hot gases expanded by combustion have lower density than cold ambient air, resulting in a net upward buoyancy force that carries hot exhaust away and entrains fresh oxygen-rich air into the flame base.

3. Governing Transport Equations

ZeroG Flames models combustion using coupled 2D advection-diffusion-reaction conservation equations:

Oxygen Transport:
∂Y_O2/∂t + u · ∇Y_O2 = D_O2 ∇²Y_O2 - s_O2 · ω

Fuel Transport:
∂Y_F/∂t + u · ∇Y_F = D_F ∇²Y_F - ω

Energy Conservation:
∂T/∂t + u · ∇T = α ∇²T + (Q · ω) / (ρ c_p) - L_loss

Arrhenius Kinetics:
ω = A · Y_F · Y_O2 · exp(-E_a / (R · T))

Boussinesq Buoyancy Flow:
u_total = u_forced + u_buoyancy, where F_b ∝ g · β · (T - T_amb)

4. Flame Color & Chemiluminescence

Flame colors are determined by two distinct physical mechanisms:

  • Blue Emission: Chemiluminescence from electronically excited radical species (such as CH* and C₂*) emitting discrete wavelengths (~431 nm and ~516 nm) during intense oxidation.
  • Yellow/Orange Luminosity: Continuum thermal radiation from incandescent microscopic soot particles heated to high temperatures (1500–2000 K).

In microgravity, residence times are longer, soot aggregates into distinct concentric shells, and radiative cooling can initiate low-temperature "cool flame" regimes.

5. NASA Research Heritage

This simulator draws inspiration from microgravity experiments conducted aboard the International Space Station and drop towers:

  • FLEX (Flame Extinguishment Experiment): Studied droplet combustion and discovered self-sustaining low-temperature cool flames.
  • ACME (Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments): Investigated gaseous flame structure and soot formation on the ISS.
  • SoFIE (Solid Fuel Ignition and Extinction): Evaluates material flammability for exploration spacecraft cabins.

6. Solid Fuel Pyrolysis & Wood Combustion Note

Solid fuels such as wood, composite polymers, or wiring insulation do not burn directly in their condensed solid phase. Instead, heat transfer from an ignition source drives an endothermic chemical decomposition process called pyrolysis:

Pyrolysis Mechanism: Solid Material + Thermal Radiation → Char Residue + Tar + Combustible Volatiles (CO, H₂, Hydrocarbon Gases).

These released volatiles then mix with ambient oxygen in the gas phase where combustion actually occurs. Consequently, solid fuels require coupled phase-change, condensed-phase energy balances, and gasification physics beyond our present gaseous-fuel model. We explicitly model gaseous reactants (CH₄, C₃H₈-like, C₂H₅OH) rather than pretending gaseous kinetics can represent solid wood burning.

7. The Cool Flame Phenomenon & Droplet Studies (FLEX)

During NASA's FLEX experiments on the ISS, scientists observed liquid fuel droplets apparently "extinguishing," only for combustion to continue invisibly at significantly lower temperatures (600–900 K vs. typical 1800–2200 K).

These cool flames are sustained by low-temperature hydrocarbon reaction pathways involving peroxide radical chemistry. Without buoyant convection carrying away heat and reactants, these low-temperature reaction regimes can persist quietly in microgravity, posing unique detection challenges for spacecraft fire safety.

8. Model Assumptions & Explicit Limitations

  • 2D Cartesian Grid: Simulates a vertical cross-section of a 8 cm × 8 cm combustion chamber.
  • Reduced-Order Fluid Dynamics: Implements a Boussinesq buoyancy body force coupled with lateral mass entrainment, rather than full 3D Navier-Stokes CFD.
  • Single-Step Global Arrhenius Kinetics: Solves Fuel + s·O₂ → Products + Heat with flammability cutoff rather than hundreds of elementary radical elementary steps.
  • Optically Thin Radiation: Radiation loss is approximated via Planck mean emission rather than full spectral radiative transfer equation (RTE) solving.
  • Educational Purpose: Built to illustrate fundamental physical mechanisms and qualitative transitions between buoyancy-dominated and diffusion-dominated regimes; not certified for aerospace flight hardware signoff.

Spacecraft Fire Safety & Mission Architecture

Mission-Critical Reality: Fire in a sealed spacecraft represents one of the most hazardous catastrophic mission risks. The absence of gravity fundamentally alters how fires start, spread, and can be detected or extinguished.

1. The Detection Dilemma: Smoke Without Buoyancy

On Earth, smoke rises straight toward ceiling-mounted ionization and optical smoke detectors. In microgravity, smoke has no upward buoyant force. Particulate matter remains suspended and follows internal cabin ventilation air currents.

Consequently, spacecraft smoke detectors must be installed directly inside air return ducts, where airflow filters and monitors the entire cabin volume.

2. Quiescent Smoldering & Ventilation Shock

In unventilated spacecraft compartments, an accidental ignition may consume local oxygen and appear to extinguish, while silently smoldering at high temperature. If an astronaut or automated system activates cabin fans, the sudden influx of forced oxygen can cause explosive flame reactivation.

3. Artemis Exploration Atmospheres

Future lunar and deep-space missions (such as NASA's Artemis program) utilize Exploration Atmospheres (e.g. 34% O₂ at 56 kPa / 8.2 psi) to minimize pre-breathe time before Extravehicular Activities (EVAs).

Elevated oxygen fractions significantly widen the flammability envelope of polymers and electrical components, making microgravity combustion modeling crucial for spacecraft material selection.

📋 Model Assumptions & Disclaimers

ZeroG Flames is a reduced-order educational combustion model designed to visualize the transition between buoyancy-dominated and diffusion-dominated transport.

  • 2D Cartesian finite-difference computational grid (64×64).
  • Reduced-order flow field combining Boussinesq buoyancy acceleration and forced cross-ventilation.
  • Single-step global Arrhenius chemical kinetics for gaseous fuels.
  • Linear species diffusion with pressure and temperature scaling.
  • Optically thin radiation loss approximation and ambient cooling.
  • Model predictions are educational and not certified CFD engineering standards.
